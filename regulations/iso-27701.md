# ISO/IEC 27701, Extension to ISO/IEC 27001 for Privacy Information Management (ISO 27701)

- Scope id: `iso-27701` (standard)
- Jurisdiction: International (ISO/IEC)
- Version: ISO/IEC 27701:2019 (2019-08-01)
- Official text: https://www.iso.org/standard/71670
- Layered on: iso-27001
- Requirements: 26 (11 implemented, 2 configurable, 10 facilitated, 3 out-of-scope)
- Browse: https://pryv.github.io/compliance-matrix/#/scope/iso-27701

## ISO 27701 5: Clause 5 PIMS-specific requirements extending ISO 27001

Coverage: facilitated · effort saved: medium · mode: awareness · draft

Requirement: Organization shall apply the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001
treating privacy as an information-security concern; PII
controllers and processors apply Annex A and/or B respectively.

**What Pryv does for you**

27701 extends the 27001
ISMS to cover PII handling specifically. Pryv contribution is
the same primitive set surfaced in the iso-27001 rows + the
GDPR rows; 27701 simply combines them under the privacy-
information-management label. The organization's PIMS-specific
risk treatment and statement of applicability sit on top.

**Evidence**

- derives from: iso-27001.6.1.2
- primitives: access, audit

## ISO 27701 A.7.2.1: A.7.2.1 Identify and document purpose

Coverage: facilitated · effort saved: medium · mode: storage · draft

Requirement: Organization shall identify and document the specific purposes
for which PII will be processed.

**What Pryv does for you**

Pryv pattern:
`access.clientData.purpose` records the per-access purpose at
grant time, durable + auditable. The vocabulary of purposes
(the organization's documented PIMS catalogue) is the operator's
editorial work; Pryv carries each instance forward.

**Evidence**

- derives from: gdpr.Art.5
- primitives: access, clientData

## ISO 27701 A.7.2.2: A.7.2.2 Identify lawful basis

Coverage: facilitated · effort saved: medium · mode: storage · draft

Requirement: Organization shall determine, document, and comply with the
relevant lawful basis for processing of PII.

**What Pryv does for you**

`access.clientData.lawful_basis`
records the determined basis (consent, contract, legal
obligation, etc.); for special-category PII,
`clientData.special_category_basis` records the GDPR Art. 9
paragraph or equivalent. Both travel with the access into the
audit chain.

**Evidence**

- derives from: gdpr.Art.6, gdpr.Art.9
- primitives: access, clientData

## ISO 27701 A.7.3.1: A.7.3.1 Determining and fulfilling obligations to PII principals

Coverage: implemented · effort saved: high · draft

Requirement: Organization shall determine and document its legal, regulatory,
and business obligations to PII principals related to the
processing of their PII.

**What Pryv does for you**

Pryv ships the technical fulfilment of the PII-principal-rights
obligations: the subject (PII principal) holding a personal
token reads everything via the standard API. The organization's
response procedure (timeline, identity verification, content of
response) is the policy half; Pryv responds in milliseconds.

**Evidence**

- tests: AA01, EGSQ, UZEV
- specs: EVENT.BASE, STREAM.BASE
- derives from: gdpr.Art.12, gdpr.Art.15
- primitives: access, stream, event, audit, data-types

## ISO 27701 A.7.3.4: A.7.3.4 Providing mechanism to modify or withdraw consent

Coverage: implemented · effort saved: high · draft

Requirement: Organization shall provide a mechanism for PII principals to
modify or withdraw their consent.

**What Pryv does for you**

Withdrawal = `accesses.delete`; modification = `accesses.update`
with new permissions / clientData. Both are versioned and
auditable; the prior consent state is preserved for §7
demonstrability. Cross-account (CMC) consent withdrawal uses
`consent/revoke-cmc` for symmetric teardown, and since
open-pryv.io 2.0.0-rc.36 the principal's own
accept event records each withdrawal, whichever party and path
ended it (`content.withdrawal`, server-owned, never overwritten)
`[CN58]`, `[CN59]`, `[CN60]`. See
`context/cmc-consent-primitives.md`.

**Evidence**

- tests: AA02, ACCP, CN58, CN59, CN60
- derives from: gdpr.Art.7
- primitives: access, permissions, CMC
- context notes: cmc-consent-primitives

## ISO 27701 A.7.4.3: A.7.4.3 Accuracy and quality

Coverage: implemented · effort saved: high · draft

Requirement: Organization shall ensure and document that PII is as accurate,
complete and up-to-date as is necessary for the purposes for
which it is processed throughout the lifecycle of the PII.

**What Pryv does for you**

`events.update` rectifies events (matching GDPR Art.16 mechanics),
and the audit log records who rectified which event and when. For
"throughout the lifecycle" proof of the values themselves, the
event version chain is the artefact, and it exists only on a
deployment with `versioning.forceKeepHistory: true` (off by
default; see `context/event-history.md`). The accuracy
determination + correction workflow is the controller's.

**Evidence**

- tests: PTU1
- derives from: gdpr.Art.5, gdpr.Art.16
- primitives: event
- context notes: event-history

## ISO 27701 A.7.4.5: A.7.4.5 PII de-identification and deletion at end of processing

Coverage: configurable · effort saved: medium · draft

Requirement: Organization shall either delete PII or render it in a form which
does not permit identification or re-identification of PII
principals, as soon as the original PII is no longer necessary
for the identified purpose(s).

**What Pryv does for you**

Per-user deletion via `system.users.delete` (engine-dependent
backup behaviour, same notes as GDPR Art. 17). Per-event /
per-stream deletion lives alongside for partial-deletion needs.
De-identification (Safe Harbor / Expert Determination methods) is
an application-layer transformation; Pryv stores the result.
"**Soon as the original PII is no longer necessary**" is the
operator's scheduled-retention obligation for records; Pryv
ships no TTL primitive for events and streams (whole accounts:
account auto-deletion since 2.0.0-rc.66, see detail); the
operator composes `events.get toTime=` + `events.delete` +
audit-log inactivity oracle in an external
retention job (`context/data-retention-operator-owned.md`).
`accesses.create {randomAlias:true}` reduces identification at
the access layer while processing continues: a platform-unique
routable alias replaces the username on that access, so what the
server returns to a counterparty holding it does not name the
principal nor match it against another access. It is not an
end-of-processing de-identification of the PII (the data stays
linked to the account). Since open-pryv.io 2.0.0-rc.69 the token
is accepted under its alias only, so a counterparty who guesses
the username cannot confirm it with the token (earlier releases
accepted it under every name of the account). At the end of
processing, deletion stays the mechanism.

**Detail**

Account auto-deletion, since open-pryv.io 2.0.0-rc.66
(`8521c8d5`, see gdpr.Art.5 §1(e)). For whole accounts, "no
longer necessary" is enforceable by configuration: your
inactivity preset (`account.autoDeletion.inactivity.defaultDays`,
default none) deletes plain accounts with an email address
(`[ACST14]`) after that many days without use of their personal
accesses (app accesses too only with
`account.autoDeletion.inactivity.countAppUse`, since
2.0.0-rc.81, `[ACST20]`), its clock starting the day it first applies
(`[ACXP2]`), after reminder mails (`[ACXP3]`) and at least 7 days
after the first notice (`[ACXP8]`); the PII principal may also
schedule their own account's deletion at a date or after a
period, where self-deletion is allowed (`[ACST10]`). The deletion
is `system.users.delete`'s, guards included (`[ADSW8]`).
Per-record retention and de-identification stay as above.

What the audit trail keeps after the deletion, from the release
after open-pryv.io 2.0.0-rc.90. With `audit.onUserDelete: erase`
(the default) the trail goes with the account and nothing is
written about the deletion in it `[AADL3]`. With `keep`, the kept
trail ends with `account.deleted` (who, the erasure ledger's
trigger; for a deletion by the principal, their client address and
the asking personal access; ids and fixed words only) `[AADL1]`,
`[AADL2]`, and the auto-deletion sweep's own record of the erasure
no longer holds the username `[ADSW17]`, so the records the server
itself writes in a kept trail name no account by its username (the
call rows keep what they always held). A restore of the account from a backup
under `keep` keeps that trail rather than replacing it, and adds
`account.restored` `[AARSC]`, `[AARS5]`.

**Evidence**

- tests: USAD, ACXP2, ACXP3, ACXP8, ACST10, ACST14, ADSW8, AADL1, AADL2, AADL3, ADSW17, AARS5, AARSC
- docs: customer-resources/account-auto-deletion.md
- config keys: storages.userAccountStorage.engine, user-account.delete, account.autoDeletion.inactivity.defaultDays, account.autoDeletion.reminders.minNoticeDays
- derives from: gdpr.Art.17, gdpr.Art.4, hipaa-security.164.310, hipaa-privacy.164.514, ccpa.1798.105
- primitives: event, stream, backup-restore, randomAlias, account-auto-deletion
- context notes: data-retention-operator-owned

## ISO 27701 A.7.4.6: A.7.4.6 Temporary files

Coverage: out-of-scope · draft

Requirement: Organization shall ensure that temporary files created as a
result of processing of PII are disposed of (e.g., erased,
destroyed) following documented procedures within a specified,
documented period.

**What Pryv does for you**

Temporary-file handling is an operating-system / runtime concern.
Pryv-the-software doesn't write PII to ad-hoc temp files; the
operator's host hygiene (tmpfs, /tmp cleanup, log rotation)
addresses any temp-file footprints the runtime produces.

## ISO 27701 A.7.4.9: A.7.4.9 Records of transfer of PII

Coverage: implemented · effort saved: high · draft

Requirement: Organization shall record transfers of PII to or from third
parties and ensure cooperation with those parties to support
future requests related to obligations to PII principals.

**What Pryv does for you**

Cross-party PII transfers in Pryv go through accesses (typically
CMC capability accesses or shared accesses). Each access's
`clientData` records recipient + purpose + legal basis at grant
time; audit log captures each transfer event with timestamp.
Together they constitute the §164.528-style accounting record
for transfers specifically.

**Evidence**

- tests: AT01, AT51, AT61
- derives from: gdpr.Art.30, gdpr.Art.44, hipaa-privacy.164.528
- primitives: access, clientData, audit, CMC

## ISO 27701 A.7.2.4: A.7.2.4 Obtain and record consent

Coverage: implemented · effort saved: high · draft

Requirement: Organization shall obtain and record consent from PII principals
according to the documented processes.

**What Pryv does for you**

Consent recording = `accesses.create` with permissions +
clientData carrying the consent text; cross-account consent =
`consent/request-cmc` events. The access keeps every previous
version; an event keeps its previous versions only with
`versioning.forceKeepHistory: true` (off by default), so the §7
demonstrability requirement is structural for the access and,
for the request event, holds as long as your app does not edit
it or history is on. Since open-pryv.io 2.0.0-rc.38 the principal's
cross-account accept records the channels the relationship got
(`features`, resolved from the offer, which the accept may only
narrow) `[HA43]`, `[CD28]`, an account cannot record a consent to
its own offer (`cmc-self-accept-forbidden`) `[HA46]`, `[CN67]`,
and the server-written parts of the record survive a concurrent
client update `[ESR1]`. See `context/cmc-consent-primitives.md`.

**Evidence**

- tests: AA01, ACCP, HA43, CD28, HA46, CN67, ESR1
- derives from: gdpr.Art.7, iso-27701.A.7.3.4
- primitives: access, permissions, clientData, CMC, event
- context notes: cmc-consent-primitives

## ISO 27701 B.8.4: B.8.4, Privacy by design and by default

Coverage: facilitated · effort saved: high · mode: infrastructure · draft

Requirement: Processor shall ensure privacy + data-protection design + default

**What Pryv does for you**

Pryv's architecture

structurally. See gdpr.Art.25 for the cross-link to the

## ISO 27701 B.8.5.2: B.8.5.2, Records of PII disclosures to third parties

Coverage: implemented · effort saved: high · draft

Requirement: Processor shall record disclosures of PII to third parties,
including what PII has been disclosed, to whom, and at what time.

**What Pryv does for you**

When the processor (Pryv operator) executes a third-party
disclosure (e.g., a CMC cross-platform share, a shared access
to a counterparty), the audit log records the API call with
access reference; the access's `clientData.recipient` +
`clientData.purpose` capture the "to whom" + "for what" fields.
Together they constitute B.8.5.2-compliant records.

**Evidence**

- tests: AT01, AT51, AT61
- derives from: iso-27701.A.7.4.9, hipaa-privacy.164.528, gdpr.Art.30
- primitives: audit, access, clientData, CMC
